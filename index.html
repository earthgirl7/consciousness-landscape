Fundamental Tensions

Where theories fundamentally disagree

1. Eliminativism versus Integrated Information Theory

Eliminativism ⋈ IIT

Central Tension: Does phenomenal consciousness even exist?

Eliminativism (Dennett, Frankish) denies phenomenal consciousness exists—it's an illusion. IIT (Tononi, Koch) makes consciousness fundamental and measurable with Φ (phi).

Empirical Test: If Φ reliably correlates with subjective reports, eliminativism fails. If consciousness reports are systematically unreliable, IIT's measurement is questioned.

2. Materialism versus Idealism

Materialism ⋈ Idealism

Central Tension: Ontological priority—what comes first?

Materialism: matter → consciousness. Idealism: consciousness → matter appearance.

Empirical Test: Can consciousness exist without physical substrate? Can we find consciousness-only phenomena?

3. Global Workspace versus Integrated Information

Global Workspace ⋈ IIT

Central Tension: Broadcasting versus integration

GWT (Baars, Dehaene): consciousness requires global broadcasting. IIT: consciousness IS integrated information—broadcasting not necessary.

Empirical Test: Brain stimulation to create high Φ without broadcasting = IIT correct. Broadcasting without integration = GWT correct.

4. Panpsychism versus Emergence Theories

Panpsychism ⋈ Emergence

Central Tension: The combination problem

How do micro-experiences combine into unified macro-experience? Emergence theories avoid this by having consciousness emerge from non-conscious matter.

Empirical Test: Find evidence of micro-consciousness, or demonstrate genuine emergence of consciousness from non-conscious matter.

5. Quantum versus Classical Neural Theories

Quantum ⋈ Classical

Central Tension: Decoherence timescales

Brain too warm and wet for quantum coherence (picoseconds) versus neural processing (milliseconds). Quantum theories claim it matters anyway.

Empirical Test: Measure quantum coherence in neural tissue during consciousness. Manipulate coherence and test consciousness effects.

6. Higher-Order versus First-Order Theories

Higher-Order ⋈ First-Order

Central Tension: Necessity of meta-representation

Higher-order theories: need thoughts about perceptions for consciousness. First-order: perception itself is sufficient.

Empirical Test: Patients with prefrontal damage but intact phenomenology challenge higher-order theories.

Points of Convergence

Where theories find common ground despite differences

Information Processing is Central

Converging theories: IIT, Global Workspace, Predictive Processing, Computational theories

All agree that information dynamics matter for consciousness, though they differ on specifics (integration versus broadcast versus prediction).

Testable implication: Manipulate information flow patterns and measure consciousness changes across different paradigms.

Body-Environment Coupling is Essential

Converging theories: Embodied Cognition, Enactive, Extended Mind, Predictive Processing

Consciousness requires active engagement with environment, not merely brain in isolation.

Testable implication: Compare consciousness quality in active versus passive conditions, sensory deprivation experiments.

Consciousness as Fundamental Property

Converging theories: Panpsychism, Idealism, Some Monisms, Cosmopsychism

Consciousness doesn't emerge from non-conscious matter; it's already present in some form at fundamental levels.

Testable implication: Look for consciousness markers in progressively simpler systems; determine where it "starts" (or doesn't).

Neural Activity as Necessary Condition

Converging theories: Most Materialist theories, Some Quantum theories, Non-Reductive Physicalism

Brain states reliably correlate with conscious states across all these theoretical frameworks.

Testable implication: Already well-tested through lesion studies, brain imaging, neural manipulation experiments.

Gradations in Consciousness

Converging theories: IIT, Panpsychism, Some Monisms

Consciousness is not binary but exists on a spectrum from minimal to full consciousness.

Testable implication: Measure consciousness markers across species, developmental stages, and brain states.

Empirical Predictions

Current status of testable predictions and novel hypotheses

Integrated Information Theory Mixed Evidence

Prediction: Posterior cortex "hot zone" has maximum Φ during consciousness

Test method: Measure integrated information in different brain regions using perturbational complexity index

Current evidence: PCI measures support it in some studies; others find consciousness without high posterior Φ

Next step: Direct Φ measurement in awake behaving subjects across all brain regions

Global Workspace Theory Strong Support

Prediction: Conscious perception requires widespread cortical broadcasting

Test method: Compare subliminal versus conscious stimuli activation patterns

Current evidence: fMRI and EEG studies consistently show "global ignition" for conscious but not subliminal perception

Next step: Artificially induce global broadcasting without external stimulus

Orchestrated Objective Reduction Disputed

Prediction: Anesthetics work by disrupting quantum coherence in microtubules

Test method: Measure quantum coherence in microtubules with and without anesthesia

Current evidence: Some anesthetic binding evidence but decoherence time problems remain unsolved

Next step: High-precision quantum measurements in living neural tissue

Predictive Processing Supported

Prediction: Prediction errors modulate conscious content

Test method: Manipulate predictions and measure phenomenology changes

Current evidence: Rubber hand illusion, binocular rivalry, bistable perception all show prediction effects

Next step: Dissociate prediction from consciousness more cleanly with neural manipulation

Higher-Order Theory Challenged

Prediction: Prefrontal cortex damage eliminates consciousness

Test method: Lesion studies and TMS disruption of prefrontal areas

Current evidence: Many patients with extensive prefrontal damage retain rich phenomenal consciousness

Next step: More precise targeting of specific higher-order representational areas

Panpsychism Untested

Prediction: No sharp threshold for consciousness in complexity scale

Test method: Search for consciousness markers in progressively simpler systems

Current evidence: Mostly philosophical; difficult to operationalize empirically

Next step: Develop consciousness measures applicable to very simple systems

Novel Testable Hypotheses

IIT versus GWT Adversarial Test

Hypothesis: Measure Φ and broadcasting simultaneously during binocular rivalry. If high Φ without broadcasting → IIT correct. If broadcasting without high Φ → GWT correct.

Method: Simultaneous perturbational complexity and EEG phase-locking measures during rivalry transitions

Impact: Would definitively distinguish two leading theories
Panpsychism Boundary Test

Hypothesis: If panpsychism is true, consciousness markers scale continuously without threshold. If emergence is true, there should be a discontinuity.

Method: Measure behavioral flexibility, information integration, and predictive capability from insects to mammals

Impact: Would inform the fundamental versus emergent debate
Quantum Coherence Correlation

Hypothesis: If quantum theories are correct, coherence time in neural microtubules should correlate with conscious state richness.

Method: Use nitrogen-vacancy diamond sensors to measure quantum coherence during waking, dreaming, meditation, and psychedelic states

Impact: Would validate or refute quantum role in consciousness
Embodiment Necessity Test

Hypothesis: Embodied theories predict consciousness quality depends on sensorimotor loops, not just computation.

Method: Compare phenomenology between (a) active sensorimotor engagement, (b) passive observation, (c) brain-computer interface without body movement

Impact: Would clarify role of body versus pure computation
AI Consciousness Marker Discovery

Hypothesis: If multiple theories agree on certain markers (integration, broadcasting, prediction error), systems with ALL markers likely have consciousness.

Method: Build AI systems with varying combinations of proposed markers; test for behavioral indicators of phenomenology

Impact: Would create consensus markers for machine consciousness
